ZERO TO GO-LIVE IN 30 SECONDS: HOW WE BUILT AN AUTONOMOUS AI MERCHANT ONBOARDING AND FRAUD PREVENTION AGENT

By Ritik Raj and the SentinelPay Team


INTRODUCTION: THE ONBOARDING BOTTLENECK

If you have ever tried to integrate a payment gateway like Razorpay or Stripe for your business, you already know the frustration of the waiting period. 

For developers and founders, waiting three to five business days for manual compliance verification is agonizing. Uploading tax documents, business registrations, bank details, and waiting for a human underwriter to review your website kills momentum. Across the financial technology sector, more than forty percent of prospective businesses give up and drop off simply because onboarding is too slow.

At the same time, payment aggregators face an equally massive risk. In fintech, this is known as a bait and switch attack. A merchant registers a harmless store selling handmade crafts or clothing. Once they receive live payment gateway credentials, they secretly swap their website overnight to sell counterfeit luxury watches, fake pharmaceuticals, or illegal betting vouchers. By the time human auditing teams notice weeks later, customer complaints have poured in, card network fines have landed, and millions in fraudulent payouts have already left the system.

We built SentinelPay to solve both sides of this problem. Our goal was simple: reduce merchant onboarding from four business days to under thirty seconds, while continuously monitoring live websites for fraud without spending thousands of dollars on artificial intelligence computing bills.


HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS BEHIND THE SCENES

Traditional web servers struggle with merchant underwriting. A single verification requires checking government tax registries, crawling a merchant website using an automated browser, and running language models to analyze policy pages. Doing all this inside a standard web request would cause web pages to freeze and time out.

To fix this, we split SentinelPay into four separate services that communicate through background messages:

First is the Web Dashboard and API Gateway. Built using Next.js and FastAPI, it handles merchant signups and admin dashboards. When a merchant hits submit, the gateway creates a pending record in the database, pushes the heavy work into a background queue, and responds back in eight milliseconds. The user sees an immediate confirmation without waiting.

Second is the Message Broker, powered by RabbitMQ. This acts as the central coordinator. It stores tasks in queues so web servers never get overwhelmed even if hundreds of merchants apply at the exact same moment.

Third is the Distributed Worker Pool, built with Celery and Python. These background workers pull tasks from RabbitMQ and perform the heavy lifting: verifying government databases, launching headless browsers to crawl sites, and running artificial intelligence models.

Fourth is the Notification Service, built with Node.js. It listens for completed decisions from the message broker and instantly emails the business owner their approval status along with an underwriting summary.


DAY ZERO: THE THIRTY-SECOND INSTANT APPROVAL PIPELINE

When an applicant submits their details, our background worker carries out an automated seventeen-step check:

Step 1: Cross-referencing government records.
We verify that the business details actually match official records. In India, a business GST number contains its tax PAN number inside it. Our code checks that these numbers match perfectly. We also verify entity types: for example, if an applicant claims to be a private limited company, their tax number must reflect a corporate entity rather than an individual person. We also verify corporate registry numbers and bank codes, checking whether multiple different businesses share the exact same bank account to detect fraud rings.

Step 2: Automated website auditing.
Next, our automated browser crawls the merchant domain. It checks whether the website is online, scans for required legal disclosures such as refund policies, shipping policies, terms of service, and privacy policies, and checks for valid customer support contacts. It also flags websites that hide behind placeholder dummy text.

Step 3: Calculating the Trust Score.
All findings feed into a scoring engine that awards a Trust Score from zero to one hundred. 
If the score is eighty-five or higher, the merchant is approved instantly. Live payment gateway credentials are generated automatically, and an email is sent out.
If the score is between sixty and eighty-four, the merchant receives a temporary forty-eight hour window to fix minor missing items, such as adding a missing refund policy link.
If the score falls below forty, or if prohibited goods like counterfeit clones are discovered, the application is rejected.


DAY N: CONTINUOUS MONITORING AND THE FOUR-TIER TRICK

Approving a merchant on their first day is only half the battle. What happens weeks later if they change their store to sell illegal items?

Here is the dilemma: if a payment company has one hundred thousand active merchants and checks every single website daily using artificial intelligence, the AI bill alone would cost thirty thousand to sixty thousand dollars every single month.

Moreover, ninety-five percent of normal online stores do not change their product categories on any given day. Calling an expensive AI model every day just to confirm that a shoe store is still selling shoes is a complete waste of money.

To solve this, we created a four-tier cascade that checks websites from the cheapest method to the most expensive:

Tier 1 is an Availability Ping. A fast network ping checks if the website is online. If the site is down, we flag it and stop immediately. This costs zero dollars.

Tier 2 is a Digital Fingerprint Comparison. On day one, we create a mathematical SHA-256 hash of the entire website text. On periodic checks, we take a new hash and compare them. If the hashes match, not a single letter on the website has changed. The check stops right there in one millisecond. This completely filters out eighty percent of merchants with zero AI cost.

Tier 3 is Semantic Similarity. What if an online store updated a banner from Spring Sale ten percent off to Spring Sale fifteen percent off? The hash will change, but the store is still selling the exact same products. Instead of running an AI model, our code converts the words into numerical frequency vectors and calculates their cosine distance. If the distance is very small, we know the changes are purely cosmetic formatting. The check stops here. This filters out another fifteen percent of merchants for zero AI cost.

Tier 4 is Deep AI Investigation. This tier is only triggered for the remaining five percent of merchants where website content has genuinely shifted or where a merchant experienced an unusual spike in transactions. Only then does our language model read the site, compare it against the original approval, and generate a fraud report.

If fraud is caught, the system dynamically lowers the merchant daily payout limit (for example, capping it to ten thousand rupees) to minimize financial exposure, and automatically routes the incident to a human compliance officer for final review.

By short-circuiting checks through this four-tier cascade, we cut overall AI operational costs by more than ninety-five percent.


CLEAN SOFTWARE DESIGN UNDER THE HOOD

We built SentinelPay using battle-tested object-oriented software design principles:

First, the Strategy Pattern for AI Providers.
Instead of locking the project to a single vendor, our code defines an abstract interface called LLMStrategy. We implemented concrete strategies for Groq (for high-speed processing), OpenAI (for deep analysis), and an offline rule-based heuristic strategy. A central context manages these strategies and automatically falls back from one to another if an API goes down.

Second, the Strategy Pattern for Payment Gateways.
We separated our payment gateway code so that we can run with live Razorpay accounts in production or instant mock accounts during automated testing without modifying business logic.

Third, the Chain of Responsibility for Rechecks.
Each tier of our four-tier monitoring system is an independent handler linked to the next. Tier one links to tier two, tier two links to tier three, and tier three links to tier four. Each handler decides on its own whether to stop early or pass the request down the chain.

Fourth, the Template Method Pattern for Safety.
All automated agents inherit from a single base class that wraps every operation with performance timers, uniform logging, and automatic error handling.


PREVENTING AI HALLUCINATIONS

In financial services, artificial intelligence hallucinations can be disastrous. Wrongfully banning an honest merchant harms real businesses, while failing to catch a scammer leads to financial theft.

We implemented four strict safeguards:

1. We enforce strict JSON outputs. The AI model is strictly forbidden from writing free-form conversational text. It must return structured data that strictly conforms to our data models.
2. We set the AI temperature to zero. This eliminates creative randomness and produces consistent, repeatable underwriting decisions.
3. We enforce exact quote matching. For every risk claim the AI makes, it must quote the exact words from the merchant website. Our Python code programmatically searches the website text for that exact quote. If the model invents a quote that does not exist on the page, the evidence is discarded immediately.
4. Human review for serious actions. The automated agent is never permitted to permanently terminate a merchant account on its own. High-risk cases are escalated to human compliance officers with a side-by-side evidence summary.


CONCLUSION

Fintech compliance does not have to mean slow, manual, multi-day delays. By combining asynchronous microservices, automated web scraping, and mathematical filtering before calling AI models, SentinelPay proves that merchant onboarding can happen in thirty seconds without sacrificing security or blowing through computing budgets.

Speed for good merchants and continuous protection against fraud can finally go hand in hand.
